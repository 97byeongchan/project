Overview

Introduction

RelFlexformers provide a new class of efficient 3D Transformers that combine expressive relative positional encodings with subquadratic attention. By framing geometric modulation via the Non-Uniform Fourier Transform, we have shown that it is possible to incorporate a broad family of integrable RPE functions without sacrificing the computational benefits of kernelized attention. Our approach achieves a complexity of O(L log L), establishing it as a highly scalable architecture for processing large-scale, irregular point clouds that were previously bottlenecked by the quadratic cost of standard dense attention. Our empirical results across various 3D benchmarks demonstrate that the flexibility provided by our framework, specifically the ability to handle non-structured, heterogeneous token distributions, leads to consistent performance gains over the baseline Performer and even surpassing the dense Transformers.

Problem

Why efficient attention needs geometry

Standard dense attention can naturally incorporate pairwise relative positional encodings, but its quadratic cost becomes a bottleneck for long 3D token sequences. Performer-style efficient attention reduces the attention cost, but it does not explicitly materialize the full attention matrix, making arbitrary pairwise RPE masks hard to insert directly.

Dense Transformer

Geometry-aware through pairwise RPE, but requires full O(L^2) attention computation.

Performer

Efficient O(L) kernelized attention, but arbitrary RPE masks are not directly available as element-wise corrections.

RelFlexformer

Uses fast mask-vector multiplication to combine efficient attention with flexible geometric modulation.

Method

Key idea: RPE mask as a fast Fourier operator

RelFlexformer writes the relative-position mask as a function of relative token coordinates. For token positions r_i and r_j, the mask is

Mij = f(ri - rj), where f is an integrable geometric modulation function.

Directly constructing M is quadratic. Instead, the paper reduces efficient RPE attention to repeatedly computing mask-vector products Mu and implements those products with forward NU-FFT, Fourier-domain modulation, and inverse NU-FFT.

Mu ~= inverse NU-FFT( Fourier(f) * NU-FFT(u) ).
Flexible RPE

One framework, many geometric priors

Different choices of the modulation function f induce different attention biases. This lets RelFlexformer express distance-decay, oscillatory, or learnable geometry without changing the underlying NU-FFT mask operator.

RoPE-style

Harmonic modulation can be represented through frequency components.

STRING-style

Projection-based cosine modulation can be handled as a structured choice of f.

Distance kernels

Radial or decay-based kernels encode locality in continuous 3D space.

RelFlexformer

Generalizes these ideas to irregular 3D token configurations.

Efficiency

Geometry-aware attention without quadratic mask construction

The cost trade-off is explicit: RelFlexformer introduces a logarithmic overhead over vanilla Performer in exchange for flexible relative positional encoding. The critical operation is FastMult, which applies the RPE mask-vector product without constructing the full pairwise mask.

Ablation

How much quadrature is enough?

The quadrature size S controls the approximation budget of the Fourier-based mask-vector multiplication. Increasing S improves the approximation only up to a task-dependent saturation point, so the page should communicate this as a practical tuning knob rather than a universally larger-is-better parameter.

Small S

Lower computational cost, but weaker approximation of the RPE operator.

Moderate S

Often enough to recover most of the gain from flexible relative positional encoding.

Large S

More accurate approximation, but may show diminishing returns depending on the task.

Scope

What this page should claim

RelFlexformer is designed to recover geometry-aware attention in efficient Transformer settings. Dense softmax attention can still be competitive on some benchmarks, but RelFlexformer significantly improves Performer-style attention while preserving subquadratic scalability.
